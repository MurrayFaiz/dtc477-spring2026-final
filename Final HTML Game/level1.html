Mission Debrief: 1
The Peril of Extinction

Essential Information:

Based off of observations from the Hubble Space Telescope, it is estimated that there are approximately 2 trillion galaxies in the observable universe. When observing distant objects in the universe, we are actually observing those objects as they appeared in the past.

The Goldilocks zone in our solar system means that Earth exists within a habitable zone around our sun, where conditions are just right for liquid water to exist on the planet's surface.

Earth and its Mass Extinctions can be broken down into the Ordovician, Devonian, Permian, Triassic, and Cretaceous.

Although not technically considered a mass extinction event, the Great Oxidation Event occurred during the Proterozoic when photosynthetic life began to rapidly produce oxygen.

The observable universe is vast and full of endless possibilities. Due to the large distances between galaxies and the finite speed of light, we are unable to directly observe the universe in real time. The further away an object is, the further back in time we observe it.

The Earth is located in the 'Goldilocks zone' — a habitable band around our sun where conditions are just right for liquid water to exist on the surface. Water is essential for the chemical reactions necessary for biological life, though methane-based life is also considered plausible.

Our planet has existed for over four and a half billion years. Mass extinction events occur when conditions become inhospitable for most species. The five main events are the Ordovician, Devonian, Permian, Triassic, and Cretaceous — the last of which wiped out the dinosaurs.

The Great Oxidation Event occurred during the Proterozoic when photosynthetic life began producing oxygen at levels toxic to the anaerobic microbial life that dominated Earth at the time — long before most life as we know it existed.
