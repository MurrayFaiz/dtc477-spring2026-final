Mission Debrief: 2
Games People Play

Essential Information:

Game Theory is the mathematical study of strategic decision-making among rational agents whose outcomes depend on each other's choices.

A zero sum game is a game where a player must defeat all other players and compete where there can only be one winner. However, in a non-zero sum game cooperation is not only encouraged, it often becomes necessary to succeed where there can be many different winners.

John Von Neumann refers to a Mixed Strategy as a strategy that randomly selects from available pure strategies according to a probability distribution, preventing opponents from predicting your moves.

The Tragedy of the Commons refers to a phenomenon in which individual users, each acting in their own self-interest, deplete a shared resource — even when doing so is against everyone's long-term collective interest.

Norbert Wiener in *Cybernetics* refers to Feedback Loops as circular processes in which a system's output is fed back as input, allowing the system to self-regulate, adapt, and correct toward a goal.

This applies not just to video games or everyday social interactions, but to larger complex systems of rewards, punishments, motivations, and feedback loops.

A mixed strategy prevents opponents from predicting your moves.

A shared resource can be managed sustainably, but becomes depleted when individual interest overpowers collective well-being.

Feedback loops appear in ecology, social systems, and even chemical processes.
