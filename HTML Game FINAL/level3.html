Mission Debrief: 3
The Dark Forest

Essential Information:

Perhaps ever since the moment humanity has gained consciousness, we have wondered whether intelligent life exists elsewhere in the universe.

According to Enrico Fermi, the Fermi Paradox highlights the conflict between the probability of intelligent life and the lack of evidence for it.

There are two main concepts to explain this: the Principle of Mediocrity (Earth is common) and the Rare Earth Hypothesis (Earth is extremely unusual).

In 1966, Robin Hanson introduced The Great Filter, a barrier that prevents life from becoming space‑faring.

The Dark Forest Hypothesis suggests civilizations hide to avoid annihilation, while the Berserker Hypothesis imagines self‑replicating probes that destroy life indiscriminately.

We have not detected signs of intelligent life elsewhere, despite the vastness of the universe.

The Principle of Mediocrity argues Earth is typical; the Rare Earth Hypothesis argues the opposite.

The Great Filter may be a catastrophic barrier that prevents civilizations from advancing.

The Dark Forest Hypothesis suggests civilizations stay hidden to avoid destruction.

The Berserker Hypothesis imagines self‑replicating machines that eliminate life preemptively.
